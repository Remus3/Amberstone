## Ambiguities I resolved

1. **The eight `prevention` values are NAMED but not DEFINED in the instrument.**
   Section 2 lists them and delegates their definitions to "v1.2's eight
   definitions", which is not in this file and which I was not permitted to go
   outside the file to fetch. I declared working definitions from the names
   themselves and applied them uniformly: GATE-EXISTING = a standing mechanised
   check existed that could have caught this AS IT STOOD and was not consulted;
   GATE-ABSENT = no such check existed, or an existing one could not have seen
   the defect without being rewritten (the section 2 tie-breaker, applied first);
   GATE-FIRED-IGNORED = a check signalled and the signal was disregarded;
   GATE-FIRED-CAUGHT = a check signalled and was the refuter; CONTRACT = a NAMED
   stated rule, fence, spec or convention was violated; CONTRACT-MISFIRED = such a
   rule was honoured and was itself wrong or expired; PROXY-MEASURE = a quantity
   was measured through a stand-in rather than directly; ADVERSARY = only a reader
   could have caught it.

2. **The set-of-values rule could make almost every row a SPLIT.** Under a
   generous reading, any row with no firing gate also supports ADVERSARY, so every
   PROXY-MEASURE row would straddle. Section 8 names an implausibly high SPLIT
   count as evidence against the convention, so I declared ADVERSARY a RESIDUAL:
   assigned only where no other value applies. Under that resolution GATE-ABSENT
   and ADVERSARY are near-disjoint - GATE-ABSENT is mechanisable-but-missing,
   ADVERSARY is not-mechanisable. Three rows carry a multi-value set
   (chunk1-07, chunk2-10, chunk2-42) and two of those straddle the family
   boundary.

3. **CONTRACT would otherwise apply to nearly every row.** RC carries standing
   directives ("verify before you assert", the recall gate) that are implicated by
   most of these defects. I narrowed CONTRACT to rows whose own text NAMES or
   directly implicates a specific rule, fence, spec or convention. This is why
   chunk4-07 is GATE-ABSENT rather than CONTRACT, and chunk2-42 and chunk4-17 are
   not.

4. **Whether a loop DIRECTIVE or a DRAFT is "filed or tracked" for `origin_time`.**
   Section 3 keys on whether the claim was "already committed, filed, or sitting in
   a tracked artifact" at refutation, and section 3's boundary makes an in-session
   subagent report FRESH. I resolved: a loop directive or dispatch instruction is a
   TRACKED artifact (INHERITED); an in-session draft, first phrasing, audit body,
   builder report or verifier report is FRESH. chunk3-01, chunk3-04, chunk4-07 and
   chunk3-11 turn on this.

5. **OVER-GENERALISED has no FRESH counterpart.** Section 3 offers the three
   sub-values only under INHERITED. chunk1-51's defect is squarely
   over-generalisation but the claim was not committed at refutation, so FRESH won
   and the sub-value was simply unavailable. I recorded the tension rather than
   forcing INHERITED to reach the sub-value.

6. **Whether a NEWLY WRITTEN test or a tool UNDER CONSTRUCTION counts as "a named
   mechanised instrument with a firing verb".** Section 2's strict reading names
   "the test suite" without qualifying it by age. I resolved that it does - so
   chunk1-31 (the arm's own first run went red) and chunk2-33 (the structural arm's
   live run) are GATE-FIRED-CAUGHT. The opposite resolution would move both to
   GATE-ABSENT.

7. **`fix_chain` across rows versus within a row.** Section 4 gives a forward
   reading and a floor rule but does not say whether a forward link established by
   a DIFFERENT row in the same corpus counts. I resolved that it does, because the
   floor rule forbids guessing upward and this is not a guess - it is a recorded
   link. chunk2-23 is scored 1 on that basis. chunk1-24 is scored 1 rather than 2
   because its third act CONFIRMS rather than refutes, and the floor rule resolves
   that in the downward direction.

8. **chunk2-45 is a refuted REMEDY, which section 1 says is a LINK and not an
   event.** I did not drop it or renumber the corpus - the task is to score the 60
   rows as filed - but I recorded that the instrument's own individuation rule
   would move it off the event denominator and onto its parent.
