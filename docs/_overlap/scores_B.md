## Ambiguities I resolved

1. **The eight `prevention` definitions are not in the instrument.** Section 2
   says to grade by "v1.2's eight definitions" but names only the eight VALUES.
   I was told to use no other file, so I derived working definitions from the
   value names plus the convention's own tie-breaker and strict-standing-check
   clause, and applied them uniformly: GATE-EXISTING = a standing instrument
   existed, could see this defect as written, and was not consulted;
   GATE-ABSENT = no such instrument, but one is constructible because the
   defect is mechanically detectable; GATE-FIRED-IGNORED = an instrument
   produced a signal that was not carried; GATE-FIRED-CAUGHT = a named
   mechanised instrument with a firing verb actually caught it; CONTRACT = a
   stated standing rule would have prevented it and was not followed;
   CONTRACT-MISFIRED = a stated rule was followed and following it caused the
   defect; PROXY-MEASURE = a proxy quantity was taken for the target quantity;
   ADVERSARY = not mechanisable, only a hostile reader prevents it. This is the
   single largest discretion in this scoring and every row depends on it.

2. **GATE-ABSENT and ADVERSARY overlap almost everywhere if read literally.**
   Nearly every row with no standing gate also has a hostile reader as its
   refuter, which would make most rows SPLIT and hollow out the SPLIT count the
   convention publishes as a check on itself. I made them mutually exclusive
   with one test: could a named mechanised instrument, if written, have caught
   this defect? Yes -> GATE-ABSENT. No -> ADVERSARY. The refuter's identity is
   never what decides it, per section 2's rule that what a reader FOUND is
   graded on its own merits.

3. **PROXY-MEASURE against GATE-ABSENT.** These straddle the family boundary, so
   assigning both manufactures a SPLIT. I used PROXY-MEASURE only where the row
   text itself shows one quantity standing in for another (synthetic for real,
   chars for bytes, comment for setting, grep for registry, path census for
   live-state mutation). Where the defect is an ordinary wrong count, missing
   site or stale citation, I used GATE-ABSENT alone. Rows chunk1-41, chunk3-44
   and chunk1-24 were the close calls and I resolved all three to GATE-ABSENT.

4. **Section 3 gives no sub-value guidance for FRESH.** The three sub-values are
   required on INHERITED only, so an over-broad claim produced this session
   (chunk4-20, chunk1-51) has nowhere to record that character on the origin
   axis. I left FRESH bare and let the over-breadth show in `prevention`.

5. **BORN-WRONG against DECAYED is undetermined where the row gives no time
   evidence.** The convention states the FRESH/INHERITED test precisely and the
   sub-value test not at all. I defaulted to BORN-WRONG unless the row's own
   text time-indexes the falsity ("already content-stale", "pre-existing stale
   prose", "now stale", "FALSE AT HEAD", a corpus that grew between filing and
   measurement). Rows chunk4-10, chunk1-41, chunk1-47 and chunk1-57 are the ones
   this rule decided rather than the text.

6. **The FLOOR rule in section 4 covers 0 but not 1.** It says a chain of 0 with
   no established forward link is scored down, and says nothing about scoring a
   1 down from a possible 2. I extended it in the same direction: where a third
   act reframes two earlier figures rather than refuting the second
   (chunk1-24), I scored 1 rather than 2.

7. **"Filed" is not defined for in-session dispatch artifacts.** Section 3 makes
   a subagent report FRESH and a committed or tracked claim INHERITED, but a
   directive, dispatch or override sits between. I treated a loop directive as
   INHERITED (it is a tracked artifact the executor receives) and a dispatch
   note, override or adjudicator statement produced and consumed inside the same
   session as FRESH.
